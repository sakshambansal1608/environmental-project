Why this matters

Groundwater is India's hidden water supply, and it's being drawn down faster than rain can refill it.

Most Indian cities lean on groundwater when piped supply falls short. When extraction keeps outpacing recharge, water tables drop, borewells go deeper, and water quality can worsen.

Methodology

Data sources and method

What this prototype shows, where the real numbers come from, and how to replace demonstration values with official data.

Availability

How much groundwater is recharged each year and can be extracted (CGWB/INGRES, in ham or bcm).

Extraction

How much is pumped compared with what's available — the "stage of extraction" that sets Safe → Over-exploited.

Quality

Whether water meets BIS IS 10500 limits (fluoride, nitrate, arsenic, salinity, etc.). Plenty of water can still be unsafe.

Supply shortage

A municipal gap between demand and piped supply. A city can face shortages even where aquifers are healthy.

How groundwater categories work

CGWB and state departments assess each assessment unit (block, mandal or taluk). The stage of extraction is annual extraction ÷ annual extractable resource × 100. Units are classed Safe (≤70%), Semi-critical (70–90%), Critical (90–100%) or Over-exploited (>100%), with water-level trends also considered. Cities often span several units, so a single "city category" is a simplification.

What's demonstration data here

Every number tagged DEMO is a placeholder for the prototype: city categories, depth-to-water series, rainfall series and demand–supply gaps. The sourced statistics in the "Why this matters" strip and the 135 L/person/day benchmark are real. Nothing on this page is live data.

Replacing demo data

All values sit in one CITIES data object in the page script. Download unit-wise results from INGRES, water-level observations from India-WRIS, and district rainfall from IMD; then update each city's gw, rain, cat and source fields with the year and geographic unit.

Limitations

The India outline is a simplified schematic, not a survey boundary. Water-quality notes are qualitative context, not measured values. The 3D cutaway is a conceptual teaching model. The globe is stylised rather than a satellite texture.
